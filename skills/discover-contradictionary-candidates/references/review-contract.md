# Candidate review contract

## Evidence ladder

1. **Nomination** — a public list, contributor, or one local example suggests a
   possible pattern.
2. **Local recurrence** — the move appears across distinct authored messages.
3. **Contextual boundary** — reviewers can distinguish the targeted move from
   ordinary grammar, quotations, required terminology, and task-specific form.
4. **Counterexample test** — known acceptable uses remain acceptable.
5. **Prospective observation** — the proposed interpretation predicts later
   examples without being rewritten after each hit.
6. **Promotion decision** — an authorized reviewer selects `observe`, `log`, or
   `block` and records why.

Frequency alone stops at step 2.

## Candidate families

Public discussions and local reviews commonly nominate:

- negated contrast and “not just” packaging;
- hedge, transition, and tidy resolution openers;
- tricolons and definition-style lists;
- repeated em-dash connectors;
- generic appraisal without contextual evidence;
- presence silently upgraded to proof or recovery;
- absence silently upgraded to inability;
- “fixed,” “done,” or “blocked” without a scoped receipt;
- “obviously,” “clearly,” “just,” and prescriptive “you need to” when they
  substitute for evidence, cost, or preference.

The lexical items are search handles. The semantic move is the candidate.

## False-positive record

For every candidate under observation, retain:

- attributed source or local receipt;
- target behavior in plain language;
- at least two positive examples from distinct messages;
- at least two acceptable counterexamples when available;
- known quotation, room, task, and audience confounds;
- what evidence changes the verdict;
- whether explaining the match retriggers the rule;
- safe explanation and audited override paths;
- current disposition and reviewer.

If a contributor cannot safely quote a blocked phrase, preserve the attributed
failure shape without demanding the literal text.

## Ongoing research

Re-run discovery periodically and after meaningful context changes. Survey new
public candidates, but record source quality and disagreement rather than
treating popularity as validation. Compare like-for-like windows when looking
for drift: task mix, message length, room norms, and mutual imitation can move
rates without any underlying model change.
